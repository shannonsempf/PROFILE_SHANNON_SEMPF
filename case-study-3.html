Who is the Animal
Rehoming Service?

The Animal Rehoming Service (TARS) is a Not-for-profit organisation started by Michaela Newell in 2000. TARS acts as a mediator between people looking to rehome their animal and those looking to adopt. In addition to this, TARS provides veternary care for animals who are homeless.

Why redesign the current website?

The current ‘Animal Rehoming Service’ website is significantly outdated. The directors of the organisation are concerned that the current website design may be deterring people from using the website, thereby reducing the ability of ‘The Animal Rehoming Service’ to achive their goal of reducing animal homelessness.

The project goal?

To determine how we might best assist ‘The Animal Rehoming Service’ in achieving their goal of decreasing animal homelessness.

User Research
In order to determine what direction, we should go in with the website redesign, as a group we decided to first undertake research. The main aim of our research was to understand what factors deter users from donating to, an adopting from web-based adoption agencies.

User interviews

User interview &
survey findings

The findings of our user interviews and surveys can be summaried by the following three key points.
1
Trustworthiness & Reputation Users are less likely to adopt from & donate to web-based adoption agencies that are not reputable or that they perceive to be un-trustworthy. 100% of those tested stated that they would be less likely to adopt from an outdated website, or a website that they determined as being poorly designed.
2
Access to information Users value information. Potential adopters want access to as much information as possible, upfront, about their potential new pet. “Websites with limited information make me nervous about adopting”
3
Removing hurdles to adoption Potential adopters want the adoption process to be as straight forward and easy as possible. Majority of users prefer to apply for adoption online. However many still want to have the option of talking to a real person if they wish.
Heuristic Evalutaion
In addition to user testing, a heuristic evaluation was completed on the original website to identify potential usability issues & design inconsistencies.

WCAG standards

All text to background colour combinations on the original website were tested against WCAG compliance standards. A number of these combinations did not meet ‘AA’ & ‘AAA’ WCAG standards.
Competitor Analysis
Competitor analysis was completed in order to identify any potential quick wins or key features that competitor websites have adopted but TARS haven’t.

Solutions

By completing this research we were able to come up with three potential solutions to the problem “How might we assist TARS in achieving their goal of helping pets in need find their forever homes.”
1
Build trust in the organisation by redesigning a clean & professional website
2
Providing all the information users need to make an adoption decision online.

Responsive Design
One of the issues highlighted with the original website was the lack of responsiveness across screen sizes. Due to this, we chose to design a responsive website that would work across multiple device types. This was primarily achieved by using a responsive grid system.  
Limitations & Future Considerations
Due to time constraints, we were unable to implement all of the ideas we had for the website. Given more time, some possible improvements could include:
  • Filters to cut down on user search times
  • The option to register for an account
  • Ability to save / favourite pets
  • Adoption pre-verification
  • Resources on pet care
